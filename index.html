Service-desk RAG assistant

An assistant for the service desk that answers support questions from several hundred technical manuals, so an operator gets the right procedure without searching for it.

The manuals are split into paragraphs following an internal procedure, which makes retrieval more accurate. Every question is searched twice, by meaning and by keywords, and the passages found go through a final ranking. The answer is detailed and precise, and it is built only from what the manuals say, to avoid hallucinations.

Internal RAG platform and MCP

An indexed codebase: the code is converted into documents, so that questions about it can be asked in plain language.

Every question runs a double search, on the documents and on the code. The same procedure can be called through a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server and its tools. Answers adapt to the person asking: a consultant gets a different answer from a developer, and not everyone is shown the code. They also adapt to the type of request, such as an impact analysis or documentation.

  • .NET

Tool-calling orchestration for the ERP

A tool-calling agent for the ERP: its data can be queried and edited in natural language, without knowing the tables behind it.

The available functions are not hard-coded: they are discovered dynamically at request time. The actions are the usual database operations on tables and business objects: view, add, edit, delete and count records. Deleting always asks for a confirmation first.

Self-hosted inference

The language models are from the Qwen family and are served with vLLM. All the systems here use it, with cloud models as a fallback.

Research Intern, Deep Learning

University of Padua · MSc thesis, supervised by Prof. Loris Nanni

Thesis: Neural Network Approaches for Classifying Variable Star Light Curves.

  • Reviewed recent work on classifying astronomical time series and built two PyTorch models for Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) light curves: a 2D-CNN on image-like representations of the curves and a hybrid CNN-LSTM on the reconstructed sequences.
  • With 5-fold cross-validation, the 2D-CNN reached 99.95% accuracy (F1 0.9995) on the full 5-class dataset, 94.6% on a balanced 5-class subset and 88.7% on 10 classes.
  • The CNN-LSTM reached 90.0% on the full dataset while training about three times faster, which makes it the better option when compute is limited.
  • PyTorch

  • CNN-LSTM
  • Time series
